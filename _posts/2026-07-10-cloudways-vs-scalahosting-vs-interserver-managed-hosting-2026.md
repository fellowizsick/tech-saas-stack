## What "Managed" Means Here

Before diving into the comparison, it's worth clarifying what "managed" means for each provider — because they approach it differently.

**Cloudways** is a managed cloud platform. You pick your cloud provider (DigitalOcean, Vultr, AWS, Google Cloud, or Linode), and Cloudways handles the server setup, caching, CDN, security patching, and monitoring on top of it. You don't touch the underlying server. It's <em>managed by the platform</em>, not by a support team that logs into your server.

**ScalaHosting** offers managed VPS hosting with their proprietary SPanel control panel. Their support team actively manages the server — they handle OS updates, security patches, monitoring, and can do hands-on troubleshooting. It's <em>managed by a team</em>, closer to traditional managed hosting.

**InterServer** provides shared, VPS, and dedicated hosting with a unique price-lock guarantee. Their standard shared hosting includes some managed features (free site migration, 24/7 support), but their VPS plans are unmanaged unless you pay extra. For the purposes of this comparison, we're looking at InterServer's standard shared hosting as the entry point.

## Cloudways Deep Dive — Scalable Cloud Performance

<a href="https://www.cloudways.com/en/?id=2179745" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">Cloudways</a> takes a different approach from traditional hosts. Instead of offering pre-configured shared or VPS plans, they give you a platform layer that sits on top of cloud infrastructure from DigitalOcean, Vultr, AWS, Google Cloud, and Linode. What this means in practice: you get cloud-level performance without having to manage a server yourself.

**What makes Cloudways stand out is their ThunderStack.** This is their optimized tech stack that includes Nginx, Apache, PHP-FPM, Redis, Varnish, and MySQL/MariaDB. Every server runs this stack out of the box, which means a WordPress site on Cloudways typically loads faster than the same site on a generic VPS. Their benchmarks consistently show sub-second TTFB on entry-level plans.

The <strong>Cloudflare Enterprise CDN</strong> is included at no extra cost on all plans. Most hosts charge $20+/mo for Cloudflare Enterprise access. Cloudways includes it at $14/mo. That's a significant value — automatic image optimization, global CDN caching, WAF rules, and Argo Smart Routing are all part of the deal.

**The catch:** Cloudways requires more comfort with server concepts than a traditional shared host. You configure PHP versions, set up staging sites, and manage your own caching settings. Their support team helps with infrastructure issues (server down, PHP errors), but they won't log into WordPress and fix a broken plugin. If you want a true hands-off experience, this may not be the right fit.

Best suited for site owners who've outgrown shared hosting and want cloud performance without hiring a sysadmin.

## ScalaHosting Deep Dive — VPS Power, SPanel Simplicity

<a href="https://scalahosting.com/" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">ScalaHosting</a> fills a specific gap in the hosting market: they offer managed VPS hosting with a proprietary control panel (SPanel) that they developed because they weren't satisfied with cPanel's pricing and limitations. Their managed VPS starts at $29.95/mo, which puts them in a different pricing tier than Cloudways but includes hands-on server management.

**SPanel is the centerpiece.** It's a full cPanel alternative that includes email management, DNS zone editing, file manager, backups, WordPress manager, and a security suite. The WordPress manager can deploy, clone, and stage sites with a few clicks. Because ScalaHosting controls the panel code, updates and security patches happen faster than with cPanel, and there's no per-account licensing fee — that savings gets passed to you.

The managed VPS comes with proactive monitoring and support. Their team installs OS and security patches, monitors server health, and can intervene if something goes wrong. This is closer to what most people imagine when they hear "managed hosting" — a team that watches your back.

**The catch:** The entry price ($29.95/mo) is higher than both Cloudways and InterServer. You're paying for a VPS with a dedicated allocation of resources, not a shared environment. If you're running a single low-traffic blog, this is overkill. But if you manage multiple client sites or run a resource-intensive application, the dedicated resources and SPanel management make the price reasonable.

Best suited for site owners who need VPS-level performance and want a control panel that doesn't cost extra, with a support team that handles server maintenance.

## InterServer Deep Dive — Price-Lock Budget Champion

<a href="https://www.interserver.net/r/1155259" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">InterServer</a> is the budget option in this comparison, but it's not the <em>limited</em> budget option. Their standard shared hosting plan is $2.50/mo — and that price does not increase at renewal. No intro-to-renewal jump, no fine print about "starting at" rates. What you see is what you pay, indefinitely.

The plan includes unlimited SSD storage, unlimited bandwidth, unlimited websites, free SSL, free site migration, and a free Cloudflare CDN. For $2.50/mo. The feature set at this price point is hard to beat. InterServer has been around since 1999 and owns their own data center, which keeps their cost structure stable — they're not reselling another provider's hosting at inflated margins.

**Where InterServer falls short** is performance and advanced features. Their shared hosting runs on standard Apache with LiteSpeed caching, which handles most WordPress sites fine out of the box. But if your site grows beyond a few thousand daily visitors, you'll start noticing slowdowns. There's no built-in staging environment on shared plans, no dedicated Redis server, and no choice of data centers beyond their two US locations.

Their customer support is responsive via 24/7 chat, phone, and email. Response times are usually under 5 minutes for chat. The support team is knowledgeable about their specific infrastructure but less helpful for WordPress-specific issues (plugin conflicts, theme problems) compared to SiteGround or WP Engine.

Best suited for new sites, personal blogs, and small business sites where budget is the primary concern and traffic is moderate.

## Customer Support

All three providers offer 24/7 support, but the experience differs:

<strong>Cloudways</strong> support is fast and technical — they're great at server-level issues (PHP memory limits, MySQL configuration, CDN setup) but less helpful for application-level problems. Average chat response: under 2 minutes.

<strong>ScalaHosting</strong> support is more hands-on. Because their VPS plans are managed, the support team can log into your server and troubleshoot. Their knowledge base also documents SPanel-specific tasks thoroughly.

<strong>InterServer</strong> support is available via phone, chat, and email. Phone support is a standout feature at this price point — most $2.50/mo hosts don't offer phone support. Their team handles the basics well but isn't equipped for deep WordPress troubleshooting.

## Control Panel Experience

<strong>Cloudways</strong> uses a custom dashboard that's clean and functional. Server management, application management, team access, and billing are all in one interface. It's not a traditional cPanel replacement — you manage your WordPress admin separately — but it's intuitive.

<strong>ScalaHosting's SPanel</strong> is the most feature-rich option here. It includes email, DNS, file management, databases, backups, and a WordPress manager. Because ScalaHosting developed it themselves, it's actively maintained and doesn't carry cPanel's licensing overhead.

<strong>InterServer</strong> uses cPanel on shared hosting, which is the industry standard. Most users who've ever had hosting know cPanel, so the learning curve is minimal. The tradeoff: cPanel licensing fees add to costs, and the interface feels dated compared to SPanel or Cloudways' dashboard.

## When to Upgrade from Shared Hosting

InterServer's shared plan handles most sites up to about 5,000 monthly visitors without issues. Beyond that, you'll notice slower admin panel response and potential resource throttling. At that point, moving to Cloudways or ScalaHosting makes sense.

Signs it's time to upgrade:
- Admin area takes more than 5 seconds to load
- You're seeing "resource limit reached" errors
- Page load times consistently exceed 3 seconds
- You need staging environments for development work
- You want server-level caching (Redis, Varnish) without jumping through hoops

Both Cloudways and ScalaHosting handle these growth scenarios well — Cloudways lets you scale vertically (upgrade server size with a few clicks), while ScalaHosting gives you a dedicated VPS with predictable resource allocation from day one.

## FAQ

<strong>Which is the cheapest option for a single WordPress site?</strong>
InterServer's $2.50/mo standard plan is the most affordable option for a single site, especially with the price-lock guarantee.

<strong>Can I host multiple WordPress sites on one plan?</strong>
Yes — all three providers allow unlimited websites on their base plans. Cloudways requires managing them on the same server, while InterServer and ScalaHosting let you add them through their control panels.

<strong>Which provider has the best performance for growing sites?</strong>
Cloudways offers the most scalable solution — you can upgrade your server resources in minutes without downtime. ScalaHosting's dedicated VPS also performs well but requires a migration to a larger plan if you outgrow it.

<strong>Do I need technical skills to use Cloudways?</strong>
More than InterServer or ScalaHosting. You'll need to understand basic server concepts like PHP versions, caching layers, and MySQL configuration. The Cloudways knowledge base is excellent, but you'll need to do some reading.

<strong>Is InterServer's price-lock guarantee real?</strong>
According to provider documentation and user reports, yes — the price you start at is the price you keep. There's no intro-to-renewal jump. This is unusual in the hosting industry and one of InterServer's strongest selling points.

<strong>Which is better for client hosting?</strong>
ScalaHosting's SPanel includes reseller features (create accounts, assign resources, white-label the panel). Cloudways works for client hosting but requires more manual setup per client. InterServer's shared plan is fine for hosting client sites but lacks the management tools pros need.

<strong>Does Cloudways offer email hosting?</strong>
No — Cloudways doesn't include email servers (they recommend using third-party services like Google Workspace or Rackspace Email). InterServer and ScalaHosting both include email with their plans.

<strong>What happens after the trial period with Cloudways?</strong>
Cloudways offers a 3-day free trial with a credit card. After the trial, you're charged the monthly rate with no commitment — you can cancel anytime. They don't use annual contracts.

The honest answer: there's no single winner here because these three providers serve different audiences with different needs.
