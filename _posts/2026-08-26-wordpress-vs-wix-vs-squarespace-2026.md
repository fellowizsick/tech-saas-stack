## WordPress — Maximum Control, Lowest Long-Term Cost

WordPress is the software that powers roughly 43% of all websites on the internet, according to W3Techs. It is free and open source — you download it, install it on any web host, and own everything from that moment on. The trade-off is that you handle (or pay a managed host to handle) the technical side: installation, updates, backups, and security.

The real advantage of WordPress is **freedom**. Thousands of free and paid themes control the design, and plugins add almost any feature you can imagine — SEO, caching, forms, membership areas, booking systems, and full ecommerce through WooCommerce. When you outgrow your host, you migrate to a better one in an afternoon. When you outgrow a theme, you switch without rebuilding content. Nothing locks you in.

The cost picture is where WordPress wins hardest. The software is free; hosting is the only bill. A solid starter plan costs **$2.50/mo on InterServer** with the price-lock guarantee, or **$2.99/mo intro pricing on SiteGround** for managed WordPress with staging and daily backups. That is a fraction of what Wix or Squarespace charge, and you keep that rate as long as you stay.

The downside is the learning curve. Setting up WordPress is not hard — our <a href="/2026/08/how-to-set-up-wordpress-site-siteground-2026/">step-by-step SiteGround setup guide</a> gets a site live in about 20 minutes — but you are responsible for keeping it updated and backed up. If you prefer zero maintenance, a managed plan like SiteGround handles updates, and Cloudways adds managed cloud infrastructure with Redis caching and staging built in.

**Who it is for:** bloggers who want SEO control, small businesses that plan to scale, anyone selling products long-term, and anyone who wants to own their site instead of renting it.

## Wix — The Most Flexible Drag-and-Drop Builder

Wix is the easiest way to build a website that does not look like a template. The drag-and-drop editor lets you place any element anywhere on the page — text, images, buttons, video, forms — with pixel-level control. For people who want a truly custom layout without touching code, Wix feels liberating compared to structured builders like Squarespace.

Wix runs on a free plan (with Wix ads and a Wix subdomain), which makes it the best platform for testing the waters. Paid plans start at **$17/mo on annual billing** for the Light plan, which removes ads, connects a custom domain, and includes 2GB of storage. The **Core plan at $29/mo** adds ecommerce, 50GB of storage, and up to five collaborators. Business plans run $39–$159/mo for advanced ecommerce, subscriptions, and priority support.

The trade-offs are real. Wix is a closed platform: your site, content, and design live on Wix's infrastructure, and you pay a subscription for as long as you want the site to exist. You cannot export your design to another host, and migrating content out later means rebuilding. Advanced SEO — custom schema, precise crawl control, server-level tweaks — is more limited than WordPress. Wix also injects its own scripts into pages, which in our testing can slow down larger sites unless you use their optimization tools diligently.

**Who it is for:** beginners who want full layout freedom, quick landing pages, event or booking sites, and anyone who wants to launch today without learning hosting.

## Squarespace — The Polished Template Experience

Squarespace is the design-first option. Its templates are consistently the most refined of any builder, with typography, spacing, and responsive behavior that look expensive out of the box. Instead of free-form drag and drop, Squarespace uses structured sections and blocks, which keeps sites clean and consistent — you are less likely to accidentally create a messy layout.

Squarespace restructured its plans in late 2025. The current lineup starts at **$16/mo on annual billing for Basic** (portfolios, blogs, personal sites), **$23/mo for Core** (small businesses, service providers, custom code access), **$39/mo for Plus** (growing ecommerce stores), and **$99/mo for Advanced** (high-volume sellers). Every annual plan includes a free custom domain for the first year, and annual billing saves 28–36% versus month-to-month.

Squarespace shines for visually driven sites: portfolios, photographers, restaurants, boutique stores, and personal brands. Its ecommerce features on Plus and Advanced are polished — inventory, subscriptions, memberships, and selling on social channels all work smoothly. The platform also handles the technical upkeep for you, so there is no hosting, updating, or security work.

The limits: like Wix, it is a closed platform with subscription pricing that continues forever, and you cannot export your design. Custom code is restricted to the Core plan and up, and advanced SEO and analytics control lag what WordPress offers with plugins. For complex stores or feature-heavy sites, you will hit the ceiling faster than you would on WordPress.

**Who it is for:** design-conscious creators, portfolio and photography sites, small stores that want a beautiful site with minimal effort, and anyone who wants zero technical maintenance.

## Ecommerce: WooCommerce vs Wix vs Squarespace

If you plan to sell products, this decision matters more than any other factor.

**WordPress + WooCommerce** is the most flexible and the cheapest to scale. WooCommerce itself is free; you pay for the extensions you actually need (payment gateways, shipping, subscriptions). Transaction fees are whatever your payment processor charges — typically 2.9% + $0.30 with Stripe or PayPal. There are thousands of plugins for memberships, bookings, dropshipping, and subscriptions, and your store is portable to any host. Our WooCommerce speed guide covers the caching and optimization setup that keeps a growing store fast.

**Wix** brings ecommerce in at the Core plan ($29/mo annual), with unlimited products, online payments, and booking tools. It is genuinely easy to set up a first store, and there are no additional platform transaction fees. The limitations show up as you grow: fewer extensions, less control over checkout flows, and higher subscription costs as you climb toward Business and Business Elite plans.

**Squarespace** offers the most polished commerce experience of the closed builders. The Plus plan ($39/mo annual) includes subscriptions, memberships, and 0% transaction fees on physical products when using Squarespace Payments. For a boutique store with beautiful product pages, it is excellent. For a store with complex shipping rules, custom tax needs, or aggressive growth, WooCommerce remains more capable and cheaper.

## SEO: Who Ranks Better on Google in 2026?

All three platforms can rank on Google — but the ceiling differs.

**WordPress gives you full control.** Plugins like Rank Math or Yoast handle meta tags, XML sitemaps, schema markup, and content analysis. You control page speed with caching plugins and image optimization, you can add any structured data, and you can implement advanced technical SEO without waiting for a platform to ship a feature. Most SEO professionals we know build client sites on WordPress for exactly this reason — our WordPress SEO setup guide walks through the full configuration.

**Wix** has improved its SEO significantly and now generates clean URLs, proper sitemaps, and decent schema. The limits are advanced control: some technical SEO tasks (server-level headers, custom redirects at scale, advanced schema) are restricted or impossible, and the platform's injected scripts can hurt Core Web Vitals on heavier pages.

**Squarespace** sits between the two — clean semantics, solid built-in SEO settings, and automatic sitemaps. Custom schema and advanced technical tweaks require code injection (Core plan and up), and you are limited to what the platform allows. For most small sites, Squarespace's built-in SEO is enough to rank for local and niche keywords.

## Decision Guide: Which Should You Choose in 2026?

Work through these questions and the answer becomes obvious:

1. **Do you want to own your site?** If yes — WordPress. If you are fine renting a platform forever — Wix or Squarespace.
2. **How much do you want to spend over 3 years?** Under $150 total — WordPress. $500–$600+ — Wix or Squarespace.
3. **Do you need maximum design flexibility?** Wix's free-form editor is the most flexible of the closed builders; WordPress with a page builder like Elementor matches or exceeds it.
4. **Do you want a beautiful site with zero maintenance?** Squarespace is the best design-per-effort ratio, followed closely by Wix.
5. **Are you building an online store you plan to grow?** WordPress + WooCommerce, without question.
6. **Is this a quick landing page or one-off project?** Wix's free plan or a cheap WordPress host both work; just avoid long subscription commitments.
7. **Do you care about long-term SEO?** WordPress gives you the most control and the highest ceiling.
